Hardwood Floor Water Damage in Olympia

It does not take long for water to change a wood floor. As the wood absorbs water along its edges and bottom face, it expands, crowds the neighboring boards and cups, leaving the center lower than the sides. Cupping alone is no reason to give up on the floor. Boards of solid wood often return to flat if they are dried during those early days. The key is pulling moisture out of the boards and the subfloor underneath at a steady, controlled pace.

Drying mats are how our team handles wet wood floors. Each mat is sealed against the floor and hooked to a vacuum unit, which pulls moisture upward through seams and boards as dehumidifiers dry the air in the room. Compared with carpet or drywall it is slower, commonly a week or more. Hold off on the sander until every board is fully dry. A cupped floor sanded early ends up crowned afterward.

What It Looks Like

  • Gaps opening between boards after a floor has dried too fast
  • Blackish or gray discoloration at board joints and fasteners
  • Planks pulling loose from the subfloor or peaking where they meet
  • Creaking and popping noises from boards that used to be quiet
  • A washboard feel underfoot from edges that stand taller than board centers
  • Swollen trim along the edge of the floor near the leak
What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Growth Beneath the Floor

No air moves and no light reaches the gap between the hardwood and the subfloor. Growth there is invisible from above and shows up first as a musty smell.

Cracks From Rushed Drying

Heavy airflow or heat dries the face of a board well ahead of its core. The result can be cracks, splits and permanent gaps between boards.

Rusting Fasteners

Nails and staples that stay wet begin to rust and lose holding power. The rust also bleeds into the wood as dark stains that sanding may not remove.

Moisture Under the Boards

Dry feeling boards can sit on a subfloor that remains damp for a long time. Leave it wet and the cupping returns or mold grows up from below.

Rugs and furnishings come off the affected boards while pooled water is vacuumed up at once. Boards swell less when the seams stop taking in water sooner.

The Damage Timeline

The first few hours

Seepage Below the Boards

Most of the water ends up beneath the boards, having run down through the joints. You can towel off the top and still have a saturated underside.

24 to 48 hours

Cupping Starts

Swelling sets in and board edges lift. Finish may turn cloudy. The bare underside of the boards and the subfloor are where mold can start.

Days 3 through 7

Boards Start Lifting

Expanding boards push harder and harder on one another. With fasteners losing their hold and boards compressing at the edges, some areas can lift away from the subfloor.

Past one week

Permanent Damage

Expect a good chance of mold below, dark staining and boards that have buckled. At this stage most of the wet area needs to be replaced rather than dried.
