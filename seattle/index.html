Restoration Challenges Seattle Homeowners Face

Water usually escapes at the same few vulnerable spots in a home. The list below covers what we are asked to clean up most for Seattle owners.

Water heaters: Over the years, mineral deposits settle in a tank and corrode the metal underneath. Look for moisture at the base, rust streaks on the jacket or a small pool that keeps coming back. If the tank ruptures, it keeps refilling and draining onto the floor until someone closes the cold supply valve. When the unit lives in an attic or interior closet, the water reaches carpets and ceilings far from it.

Roofs: Wind can lift shingles and drive rain underneath them in a single storm. Skylights, plumbing vents and chimneys are other entry points once their seals crack with age. Water travels along rafters before it drips, so the wet spot inside may be far from the hole outside. On Seattle roof leak calls, our crew removes saturated insulation, sets drying equipment and can cover the opening until a roofer arrives.

Freezing: Depending on the season, a hard overnight freeze can burst lines that were fine the day before. The first to go are hose bibs, irrigation lines and any pipe routed through an unheated space. A house left empty or a wing kept unheated faces the greatest risk. If a faucet only trickles on a freezing morning, shut off the water and call before the pipe thaws.

Backups: A drain that gurgles or several fixtures that slow down together point to a blockage in the main line. Once it closes off fully, anything you flush or drain returns at the lowest opening in the house. The water is unsanitary, and hard surfaces need disinfecting after the solids and liquid are removed. Stay out of the affected rooms and avoid running water until the line is cleared.

Seepage: A wet line along the bottom of a foundation wall is a sign that water is building up in the soil next to it. The usual reasons are grading that tilts inward, clogged perimeter drains and downspouts that empty too close. Early clues include powdery white residue on the concrete, flaking wall paint and air that always smells musty. The team at Olympia Restoration can extract standing water and dry the space, and we will tell you what we think let it in.

How Drying Works on a Seattle Water Damage Job

A surface that feels dry is not proof that a room is dry. The face of drywall often seems normal when the cavity side is damp, and carpet fibers dry long before the padding underneath. That is why professional drying runs on instruments. In Seattle we use pin meters that measure moisture inside wood and drywall, non-invasive meters that scan behind surfaces, and thermal cameras that show cool, damp patches.

Different materials let go of water at different speeds. Carpet and drywall respond quickly, while framing lumber, subfloor, hardwood and concrete hold on much longer. We place air movers and dehumidifiers according to those differences and adjust them as the daily readings change. A span of 3 to 5 days covers most jobs, and we will tell you early if yours looks like an exception.

Stopping early is the costly mistake. Damp materials sealed behind new finishes may mold, buckle or delaminate months afterward. So for every Seattle job we dry to a measured target and keep the readings on file.
