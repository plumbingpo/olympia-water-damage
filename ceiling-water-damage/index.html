Ceiling Water Damage Repair: What Olympia Homeowners Should Know

When a brown circle finally appears above you, the leak has been at work for a while. By the time the stain appears, water has already run along a joist, soaked the insulation and pooled on the back of the drywall. The leak may be a bathroom on the floor above, a pipe in the ceiling, an air conditioner drain line or the roof. We trace it to the source before anything gets patched.

A coat of paint over the spot solves nothing. Moisture left in the cavity brings the discoloration back and lets mold get going on the paper side in 24 to 48 hours. We open only what we need to, pull out wet insulation, dry the joists and remaining drywall, then patch, texture and paint so the repair blends in. Sagging changes the picture. Keep clear of that area and phone us.

What It Looks Like

  • Tan or rust colored circles that widen following a storm or a shower
  • Ceiling paint lifting off in blisters or peeling strips
  • One room that smells damp even though nothing looks wet
  • Small black or green specks appearing in a cluster overhead
  • Water trails on the upper wall, starting at the ceiling corner
  • A smoke detector, register or fixture with water running from it

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

What the Upstairs Is Hiding

Whatever is over the ceiling, a subfloor or attic decking, has typically been soaked as well. We check that level as well, because drying only the ceiling leaves moisture behind.

Electricity Overhead

Can lights, ceiling fans and electrical boxes are located exactly where the water is. Water in a fixture can short the circuit, so switch off the breaker for that room.

Mold Above You

Mold usually grows out of sight on the upper face of the panel and in the insulation above, both of which hold moisture well after the painted side dries.

Sudden Collapse

Once saturated, drywall weighs much more and no longer holds its screws well. Whatever sits under a swollen area is at risk if that section comes down.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Water Spreads Above

The insulation gets wet as water travels down the framing. Because the panel soaks from its top side, nothing may look wrong from underneath.

24 to 48 hours

Marks Start Showing

Discoloration shows up and the paint begins bubbling. Mold can begin on the drywall paper and the wet insulation presses down on the panel.

Day 3 to 7

The Ceiling Bows

The panel turns soft and starts slipping off its fasteners. A stale odor drifts into the room as seams separate and the drywall sags in the spans between framing.

After a week

Risk of Collapse

Saturated pieces may drop and carry soaked insulation down too. The repair zone is far bigger at this stage, with mold well rooted inside the cavity.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Find the Source

Using thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team follows the water to its origin and inspects the attic or room overhead. Until the water stops coming, fixing the ceiling would not last.

2

Secure the Room

We cut power to any wet fixture and move or cover the furniture underneath. A ceiling with pooled water is relieved carefully, with the water collected as it comes out.

3

Open and Dry

Damaged drywall and wet insulation come out. Air movers and a dehumidifier dry the joists and surrounding ceiling, and we apply antimicrobial where the water was not clean.

4

Rebuild and Paint

When everything reads dry, our crew replaces the insulation, hangs drywall, finishes the seams, blends the texture with the rest of the ceiling, then applies stain blocker and paint.
